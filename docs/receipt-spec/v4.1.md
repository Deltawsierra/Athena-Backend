## 1. What a receipt attests, and what it does not

In the module's own words (`assurance/receipt.py:11-19`):

> - it attests **integrity and provenance** — *this is the evidence that was
>   recorded, unaltered*;
> - it does **not** attest that the conclusion is *true*. How strongly a finding
>   is actually known is the evidence *class* (`technically_verified` down to
>   `vendor_asserted`), a separate axis this receipt never inflates. A receipt
>   over a `vendor_asserted` claim proves the claim was recorded faithfully, not
>   that the vendor is right.

and (`assurance/receipt.py:72-75`): "the receipt attests **integrity and
provenance** — *this is the assurance state that was recorded, unaltered* — and
never that the conclusions are true or the system is secure."

So, precisely:

- **A verified signature** establishes that this is the assurance state
  athena-backend recorded, unaltered since the holder of the named key signed it,
  and that the key was trusted to sign (`active`) or had been rotated out without
  being revoked (`retired`).
- **A matching digest on an unsigned copy** establishes only that the copy is
  internally consistent. A digest is a checksum anyone can recompute; it says
  nothing about who produced the receipt.
- **Neither establishes that the assessment is correct or the system safe.** A
  `needs_more_evidence` decision, or an assessment resting on `vendor_asserted`
  evidence, is carried at its true strength. A valid signature over a weak claim
  is a valid signature over a weak claim.
- **A 4.1 signed receipt says when it was issued, and no more than that.** Its
  signed form carries `issued_at` (section 4.1): athena-backend's clock at the
  moment it handed the receipt to the engine to be signed, inside the signature
  and outside `digest`. A time edited after signing fails the signature. What it
  does not establish is that the clock was right, or when the state it describes
  came to hold or stopped holding: it is the issuer's clock, not a trusted
  timestamp. Receipts are signed on read, so `issued_at` is the time of the read
  that produced this copy, not the time of any assessment. A signed `ready` from
  before a regression still verifies for ever (a retired key goes on verifying
  what it signed), but from 4.1 it says how old it is, and a reader with a
  freshness policy refuses it. How old is too old is the reader's to decide; the
  offline verifier's `--max-age` applies one (sections 7 and 9).
- **A 4.0 or older signed receipt carries no time.** Its signed form left out
  `computed_at` and put nothing in its place, and the envelope carries no time, so
  the same assurance state signed to the same bytes on any day. The verifier says
  it is `not signed (4.0)` and never shows a time for it; under `--max-age` it
  refuses it (`no_signed_time`). The times inside the digest, such as
  `coverage.checks_reported_at`, are when a measurement was reported, not when a
  receipt was issued.
- **A receipt is a reduction.** It carries counts and digests, not the rows behind
  them: no finding list, no route detail, no named coverage gaps, no workflow
  names. Those are retrievable from the deployment; the receipt binds digests of
  them (section 5.4).

## 2. Where receipts are served

Every route below is a read under `/api/assurance/`, open to any authenticated
operator (`IsAuthenticated`), computed on read and never stored.

| Route | Serves | Signed |
|---|---|---|
| `GET deployments/<uuid>/assurance-receipt/` (`assurance/views.py:662-686`) | The receipt, full form (section 4). | No, and it says so: `signed: false`, `signature: null`, `unsigned_reason`. |
| `GET deployments/<uuid>/signed-assurance-receipt/` (`assurance/views.py:688-829`) | The signed form of the same receipt, stamped with the time it was issued, beside a DSSE envelope over it, or the reason it could not be signed (section 6.6). | Yes, when the engine signs. |
| `GET deployments/<uuid>/receipt/` (`assurance/views.py:652-660`) | The bare evidence root: `algorithm`, `digest` (= `evidence.root`), `finding_count`, `computed_at`. | No, and it carries no field that says so (section 10). |
| `GET findings/`, `GET findings/<uuid>/` | Each finding's `receipt`: `algorithm`, `digest` (the leaf of section 5.3), `evidence_count`, `computed_at`. | No. |
| `GET findings/<uuid>/incident-pack/` | `identity.finding.uuid`, `identity.finding.fingerprint`, `evidence.rows` and the finding's `receipt`: everything the leaf covers (section 5.3). | No (the pack's own signing is out of scope here). |
| `GET claims/` and a deployment's `assurance-claims/` | `receipt_digest`: the top-level `digest` of the state a claim was derived from (`assurance/claims.py:1171`). | No. |

The engine serves the two routes the signature needs. `POST
/api/assurance/receipt/sign` is operator-only, and the backend calls it
(`ai_engine/services/cyberengine_client.py:406-424`). `GET /api/assurance/keyring`
is unauthenticated, and is where the engine publishes its public keyring (section
6.4). The backend does not serve the keyring.

**Who signs what.** The engine signs the document the backend hands it and builds
none of its own. Its route takes `{"receipt": P}` (athena-engine
`api/server.py:453-465`) and passes `P` to `wrap` (`api/server.py:2744-2770`), which
serialises it (section 6.2) and signs that; it adds no member and no time. So the
backend decides every byte of the signed form, `issued_at` included, and the
engine's clock appears nowhere in a receipt.

## 3. Conventions

- A receipt is JSON (RFC 8259). No object in it names a member twice.
- Every number in a receipt is an integer. None carries a fraction or an exponent.
- A digest is 64 lowercase hexadecimal characters of SHA-256.
- `issued_at` is an RFC 3339 date-time in exactly one form:
  `YYYY-MM-DDTHH:MM:SSZ`, UTC, whole seconds. Every other date-time in a receipt
  (`computed_at`, `coverage.checks_reported_at`) is ISO 8601 as Python's
  `isoformat()` writes it.
- **Not known is said, never defaulted into a pass.** Each member that can be
  unknown has one explicit way of saying so, and none of them is `0`, `true` or an
  empty list standing in for "fine":

| Member | How "not known" or "not declared" is said |
|---|---|
| `result.decision` | `null`: no decision has been computed. Never read as `ready`. |
| `policy` | `{"declared": false}` and nothing else: no data boundary was ever approved, and none is invented. |
| `served_route.unknown_fields` | Names every route field never observed. A route field nothing observed is the string `unknown`, inside the route detail the fingerprint covers. |
| `served_route.complete` | `false` when there are no routes: nothing served is not everything observed. |
| `coverage.verdict` | `undeclared`: with no declared architecture there is no promise to be short of. Its own answer, not a weak `complete`. |
| `coverage.checks_reported` | `false`: no engine said which checks it ran. Then `checks_total`, `checks_performed`, `checks_complete`, `checks_gap_fingerprint` and `checks_reported_at` are all `null`, never `0` or `false`. |
| `chains.approved_set_recorded` | `false`: nobody recorded the approved workflow set. Then `workflows_expected` is `null`. |
| `chains.rule_decision` | `null`: no chain was assessed. Never read as `ready`. |
| `chains.census` and the other censuses | Every key of the vocabulary is present, zeros included: a count that appeared only when non-zero would read like a zero when missing. |
| `signed`, `signature`, `unsigned_reason` | `false`, `null` and a sentence: this copy is unsigned, said in the payload. |
| `issued_at` | Always present in a 4.1 signed form, never unknown there. A 4.0 or older signed form has no `issued_at` at all: its time is not known, and no reader supplies one. |

## 4. The receipt, member by member

The receipt has two forms. The **full form** is what `assurance-receipt` serves.
The **signed form** is the full form without `computed_at`, `signed`, `signature`
and `unsigned_reason`, and with `issued_at`: the envelope's payload, and the
`receipt` served beside it (section 6.1). Every member listed is required in the
form it belongs to. The only optional members in a 4.1 receipt are the three inside
`policy` that are present exactly when `policy.declared` is `true`.

## 5. Canonical form and digests

### 5.1 The digest's canonical form

Every digest in a receipt is `hex(SHA-256(C(v)))`, lowercase, where `C(v)` is these
bytes, exactly (`_digest`, `assurance/receipt.py:239-244`, which is
`json.dumps(v, sort_keys=True, separators=(",", ":"))` encoded):

- **Objects**: `{`, members sorted by name (by Unicode code point; every name in a
  receipt is ASCII), each `"name":value`, separated by `,`, then `}`. No
  whitespace anywhere.
- **Arrays**: `[`, elements in their own order, separated by `,`, then `]`. Array
  order is content.
- **Strings**: `"` ... `"`. Every character outside U+0020..U+007E is escaped as
  `\uXXXX` with lowercase hex, and a character beyond U+FFFF as the two escapes of
  its UTF-16 surrogate pair. The exceptions are U+0008 `\b`, U+0009 `\t`,
  U+000A `\n`, U+000C `\f` and U+000D `\r`. `"` is `\"` and `\` is `\\`. U+007F is
  escaped (`\u007f`); `/` is not. The bytes are therefore pure ASCII.
- **Integers** in decimal: no leading zero, no `+`, `-` for negatives. **`true`**,
  **`false`**, **`null`** as written.

Example: `C({"b": 1, "a": "Zü😀"})` is the 33 bytes
`{"a":"Z\u00fc\ud83d\ude00","b":1}`.

The backend's `_digest` also passes `default=str`. It never applies to a receipt,
because every value in one is a JSON string, integer, boolean, null, array or
object. The conformance tests recompute every digest from the bytes the routes
serve.

What does not change a digest is how the JSON you hold is laid out: member order
and whitespace. What does change it is any value, any member name, array order and
any number.

### 5.2 The top-level digest

`digest = hex(SHA-256(C(H)))`, where `H` is the receipt with only these members:
`receipt_version`, `policy_version`, `system`, `result`, `policy`, `evidence`,
`assessments`, `served_route`, `coverage`, `chains`. That is every member except
`algorithm`, `digest`, `issued_at`, `computed_at`, `signed`, `signature` and
`unsigned_reason`. The rule is the same in every version: each has hashed exactly
the members it carries other than those seven (section 8).

So no clock enters the digest: two signings of one state carry one `digest`, the
same one the unsigned copy carries.

### 5.3 The evidence root

For a deployment `D` with findings `F1 ... Fn`:

1. For each finding `F`, take its evidence as rows `[classification, source,
   content_hash]`, with an empty value as `""`, and sort the rows: element by
   element, by code point (`assurance/receipt.py:274-283`).
2. `leaf(F) = hex(SHA-256(C({"evidence": rows, "fingerprint": F.fingerprint,
   "uuid": F.uuid})))` (`finding_receipt`, `assurance/receipt.py:286-302`).
3. `evidence.root = hex(SHA-256(C({"deployment": D.uuid, "findings": sorted leaf
   digests})))`, and `evidence.finding_count = n` (`deployment_receipt`,
   `assurance/receipt.py:305-321`). With no findings the root is still taken, over
   `"findings": []`, so it is still bound to the deployment.

There is no domain-separation prefix. The two levels are told apart by the member
names of the object each hashes (`evidence`, `fingerprint`, `uuid` against
`deployment`, `findings`), so a leaf's canonical bytes can never be a root's. A leaf
enters the root as its 64-character hex string.

**Getting the evidence.** List the deployment's findings with `GET
/api/assurance/findings/?deployment=<uuid>`, following every page. For each, `GET
/api/assurance/findings/<uuid>/incident-pack/` gives `identity.finding.uuid`,
`identity.finding.fingerprint` and `evidence.rows` (already sorted). Use the
incident pack: the finding list serves the evidence rows but not the fingerprint,
and the leaf covers the fingerprint. The per-finding `receipt.digest` both routes
serve is `leaf(F)`. A reader who is not an admin or analyst sees only the findings
of scans they ran or deployments they own, and a set missing one finding cannot
reproduce the root.

**The evidence file** that `tools/verify_receipt.py --evidence` reads:

```json
{
  "deployment": "<system.uuid>",
  "findings": [
    {"uuid": "<finding uuid>", "fingerprint": "<finding fingerprint>",
     "evidence": [["<classification>", "<source>", "<content_hash>"]]}
  ]
}
```

### 5.4 The other digests a receipt binds

`assessments.*`, `served_route.fingerprint` and `coverage.checks_gap_fingerprint`
are digests the backend takes, with the same canonical form, over detail the
receipt deliberately does not carry. The top-level digest and the signature bind
them. A verifier does not recompute them from the receipt, and this specification
does not define how to rebuild their inputs.

## 6. The signature

### 6.1 What is signed

The **signed form** `P` is the receipt without `computed_at`, `signed`,
`signature` and `unsigned_reason` (`NOT_SIGNED_OVER`, `assurance/receipt.py:151`),
and with `issued_at` (`SIGNED_FORM_ONLY`, `assurance/receipt.py:174`). `P` is made
by `signable_receipt` (`assurance/receipt.py:1369-1398`), which stamps `issued_at`
with the backend's clock when the signed route builds `P` and hands it to the
engine (`assurance/views.py:760`, `779`). `P` keeps `algorithm` and `digest`, so the
signature covers every hashed member, those two, and the time it was issued. The
reason for leaving the four out, in the payload's own words
(`NOT_SIGNED_OVER_REASON`, `assurance/receipt.py:156-168`):

> `computed_at` is when the unsigned copy was rendered, and nothing signs it. The
> signed copy carries its own time instead: `issued_at`, when this backend handed
> it to be signed, inside the signature and outside `digest`. So two signings of
> the SAME assurance state carry the same `digest` and differ by their `issued_at`
> alone: 'has anything changed?' is answered by comparing digests, and 'when was
> this issued?' by the signed time. `signed`, `signature` and `unsigned_reason` are
> a document's self-report about whether it is signed, which is false the moment
> it is signed -- whether an artifact carries a signature is a property of the
> envelope around it, not a claim the content can make about itself.

Two signings of one state therefore differ in `issued_at` alone, and so do their
envelopes. "Has anything changed?" is a comparison of digests, never of envelopes.

### 6.2 The payload bytes

`B = UTF-8(JSON(P))` with members sorted by name and no whitespace, and **with
non-ASCII characters raw**: only `"`, `\` and U+0000..U+001F are escaped
(athena-engine `engine/evidence/envelope.py:97-106`, `ensure_ascii=False`). This is
not the digest's form: `ü` is the two bytes `C3 BC` here and the six characters
`\u00fc` in 5.1. The signature is over `B` exactly as it arrives. A verifier never
re-serialises the payload to check a signature; it re-serialises it only to
recompute the digest.

### 6.3 Pre-authentication encoding

The bytes signed are DSSE's pre-authentication encoding (athena-engine
`engine/evidence/envelope.py:75-94`):

```
PAE = "DSSEv1" SP len(T) SP T SP len(B) SP B
```

`SP` is one space (0x20). The lengths are byte counts in ASCII decimal. `T` is
`application/vnd.mythos.assurance-receipt+json` (45 bytes), so every signed
receipt's PAE begins `DSSEv1 45 application/vnd.mythos.assurance-receipt+json `.
The type is inside the signed bytes, so a signature made over the same bytes as
another kind of document (`application/vnd.mythos.evidence-pack+json`) does not
verify as a receipt.

### 6.4 Algorithm, key identity and the keyring

- The signature is **Ed25519** (RFC 8032, pure), 64 bytes.
- A key's id is `sha256:` followed by the first 32 hex characters of SHA-256 over
  its 32-byte raw public key (athena-engine `engine/evidence/signing.py:128-134`,
  `engine/evidence/keyring.py:116`). An id is its key's digest, so one key has
  exactly one id. That is what keeps a revoked key revoked: revocation is recorded
  against the id.
- The engine publishes its **keyring** at `GET /api/assurance/keyring`. It holds
  public keys only:

```json
{"version": 1,
 "keys": {"sha256:<32 hex>": {"public_key": "<base64 of the 32 raw bytes>",
                             "status": "active", "created_at": "...",
                             "retired_at": null, "reason": null}},
 "caveat": "..."}
```

- **Statuses**: `active` signs and verifies. `retired` never signs, and verifies
  what it signed for ever, so rotating a key does not destroy published receipts.
  `revoked` verifies nothing, and every receipt signed with it is invalid.
- Fetch the keyring out of band from the receipt. A key taken from the same place
  as the artifact it checks proves only that the two agree with each other.

### 6.5 The envelope

```json
{"envelope_version": 1,
 "payloadType": "application/vnd.mythos.assurance-receipt+json",
 "payload": "<base64 of B>",
 "signatures": [{"keyid": "sha256:<32 hex>", "sig": "<base64 of the 64-byte signature>"}]}
```

Base64 is the standard alphabet with padding. The engine emits exactly one
signature (athena-engine `engine/evidence/envelope.py:120-168`). The envelope
carries no time of its own; the only signed time is `issued_at`, inside `B`.

### 6.6 The signed route's answer

Signed:

```json
{"receipt": P, "not_signed_over": {"fields": ["computed_at", "signed", "signature", "unsigned_reason"], "why": "..."},
 "signed": true, "reason": null, "envelope": E, "verify": "..."}
```

Not signed (the engine unreachable, unconfigured or refusing, or its answer not an
envelope over `P`):

```json
{"receipt": P, "not_signed_over": {...}, "signed": false, "reason": "<why, in the backend's words>", "envelope": null}
```

Only the envelope's payload is signed. `receipt` is a copy for reading, and a
verifier checks that it is the payload (step 4). `signed`, `reason`,
`not_signed_over` and `verify` are the backend's report about the envelope, and a
verifier does not rely on them. `not_signed_over.fields` names what the full form
carries and `P` does not; `issued_at`, which `P` carries and the full form does not,
is explained in its `why`. On the unsigned answer `P` still carries an `issued_at`:
the time it was handed to be signed, which nothing then signed.

### 6.7 How an unsigned receipt says so

- The full form says it in its own payload: `signed: false`, `signature: null`,
  `unsigned_reason: "<why>"`. All three are outside the digest, so the digest of a
  state is the same whether or not a signature exists, and outside the signature,
  where they would deny the signature they sit inside.
- The signed route says it beside the receipt: `signed: false`, a `reason`, and
  `envelope: null`, never an envelope with an empty signature list.
- The bare `receipt` route and the per-finding receipts are unsigned checksums with
  no field that says so (section 10).

### 6.8 What the signature does not cover

- `envelope_version` and `keyid`: outside the pre-authentication encoding, as DSSE
  defines it. `keyid` is a hint resolved against a keyring you hold, and the
  keyring's id is checked against the key's material.
- Anything outside the envelope: the route's `receipt` copy, `signed`, `reason`,
  `not_signed_over`, `verify`.
- The four members of 6.1 that the signed form leaves out. The time IS covered from
  4.1: `issued_at` is inside `B`. A 4.0 or older signed receipt covers no time.

### 6.9 What the backend checks before it serves `signed: true`

`envelope_over` (`assurance/receipt.py:1220-1351`) checks:

- the envelope is an object;
- `envelope_version` is 1 (compared with Python's `!=`, so it also accepts `true`
  and `1.0`; the verifier requires the integer 1);
- the payload type is `T`;
- there is at least one signature entry with a `keyid` and a `sig`;
- the payload is base64;
- the payload nests at most 64 levels (#111);
- the payload is JSON equal, as decoded content, to the document it sent, which
  includes the `issued_at` it stamped.

It does not check the signature: "That needs the keyring" (`assurance/receipt.py:1273-1275`).
The backend holds none. Verification is the auditor's job.

## 7. Verification algorithm

Inputs: the receipt file `A`, optionally a keyring or one public key obtained out of
band, optionally the evidence file (5.3), and optionally a maximum age in seconds:
the reader's freshness policy. The steps run in order, and the first refusal ends
verification.

1. **Read.** Decode `A` as JSON text (UTF-8, or UTF-16/32 by byte-order mark, as
   JSON readers detect it). If it nests deeper than 64 levels, refuse
   `depth_limit`. The depth is the number of arrays and objects open at the deepest
   point, with brackets inside strings not counted and a backslash in a string
   escaping the character after it. It is measured before parsing, and is the
   bound the backend puts on everything it reads from the engine
   (`MAX_JSON_DEPTH = 64`, `ai_engine/services/cyberengine_client.py:152`). If `A`
   is not JSON, names a member twice, or uses `NaN` or `Infinity`, refuse
   `malformed`.
2. **Classify.** An object with any of `envelope_version`, `payloadType`, `payload`,
   `signatures` is an envelope on its own. One with `receipt` and `envelope` is the
   signed route's answer: its `receipt` is in the signed form, and `envelope: null`
   means unsigned. One with `receipt_version` is the unsigned copy, in the full
   form. Anything else: `malformed`.
3. **Open the envelope** (when there is one). Each of these failures refuses
   `not_an_envelope`:
   - `envelope_version` is not the integer 1;
   - `payloadType` is not exactly `T`;
   - `signatures` is not a non-empty array of objects, each with a non-empty
     string `keyid` and a non-empty string `sig`;
   - `payload` is not a string of strict base64.

   Decode the payload's text as in step 1. Deeper than 64 levels refuses
   `depth_limit`. Not JSON, a member named twice, or not an object refuses
   `not_an_envelope`. Keep the payload's exact bytes `B` for step 9.
4. **Containment** (the signed route's answer). If the served `receipt` is not the
   same JSON value as the payload, refuse `different_document`. From here on, the
   receipt is the payload.
5. **Version.** If `receipt_version` is absent, not a string, or not a version
   section 8 lists, refuse `unknown_version`. Never read an unknown version as the
   nearest known one.
6. **Members.** Each of these refuses `malformed`:
   - the receipt has members other than exactly the ones its version defines for
     its form (section 8.3);
   - `algorithm` is not `sha256`;
   - `digest` is not 64 lowercase hex;
   - in the full form from 3.1, `signed` is not `false`, `signature` not `null`,
     or `unsigned_reason` empty;
   - in the signed form from 4.1, `issued_at` is not a string in the one form of
     section 3 naming a real time (a month 13, a fraction, an offset other than
     `Z` or a number are all refused);
   - any number has a fraction or an exponent.
7. **Digest.** Recompute 5.2. If it differs from `digest`, refuse
   `digest_mismatch`.
8. **Evidence** (when an evidence file is given). If its `deployment` is not
   `system.uuid`, or the root recomputed by 5.3 is not `evidence.root`, or its
   finding count is not `evidence.finding_count`, refuse `evidence_mismatch`.
   Without an evidence file the root is bound by the digest and the signature but
   not re-derived, and the verifier says so.
9. **Signature.** With no envelope, refuse `unsigned`: every check above passed,
   which shows only that the copy is consistent. With an envelope and no key given,
   the verifier cannot run (exit status 2). Otherwise, for each signature entry:
   - its `keyid` is not a key you supplied: `wrong_key`;
   - that key's status is `revoked`: `revoked_key`;
   - Ed25519 verification of the base64-decoded `sig` over `PAE(T, B)`, with the
     key's material, fails (or `sig` is not base64): `bad_signature`;
   - one entry that verifies: go on to step 10.

   When no entry verifies, the reason is the last entry's, as the engine's own
   verifier reports it. An `issued_at` edited after signing changes `B`, so it
   refuses `bad_signature` here.
10. **Freshness** (only when a maximum age is given). The time is read only now:
    before the signature checks out, it is anyone's.
    - The receipt's version carries no signed time (4.0 and older): refuse
      `no_signed_time`. Its age cannot be read, so it cannot be shown to be fresh.
    - The verifier's clock minus `issued_at` is more than the maximum age: refuse
      `stale`.
    - Otherwise, **VERIFIED**.

    An `issued_at` ahead of the verifier's clock is not refused: one of the two
    clocks is wrong, and the verifier cannot tell which. It says so (section 9).
    With no maximum age, step 10 applies no policy, and a receipt that passed step 9
    is **VERIFIED**.

The refusal list. The verifier's `REFUSALS`, and the conformance vectors, are tested
against this table:

<!-- refusals -->
| Reason | Step | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `depth_limit` | 1, 3 | The receipt, or the payload inside its envelope, nests deeper than 64 levels. It is not read. |
| `malformed` | 1, 2, 6 | Not a receipt this specification describes: unreadable JSON, a member named twice, members missing or undefined for its version, the wrong algorithm, a receipt claiming a signature of its own, an `issued_at` not in its one form, or a non-integer number. |
| `not_an_envelope` | 3 | The envelope is not a DSSE envelope over an assurance receipt: the wrong version or payload type, no signatures, or a payload that is not base64 JSON. |
| `different_document` | 4 | The receipt served beside the envelope is not the one inside it. |
| `unknown_version` | 5 | A receipt version this specification does not describe. |
| `digest_mismatch` | 7 | The digest does not match the receipt's own content. |
| `evidence_mismatch` | 8 | The evidence supplied does not reproduce the evidence root: evidence changed after the receipt was computed, or the evidence file lacks or adds a finding. |
| `unsigned` | 9 | Nothing signs this receipt. |
| `wrong_key` | 9 | The signature is not by a key you gave the verifier. |
| `revoked_key` | 9 | The signature is by a key the keyring has revoked. |
| `bad_signature` | 9 | The signature does not check out over the payload's bytes. |
| `no_signed_time` | 10 | A maximum age was given, and the receipt carries no signed issue time (4.0 and older): its age cannot be read. |

**What the backend's own code checks of this.** Steps 1 (for an envelope's payload),
3 and 4 are `envelope_over`. Step 5 is `receipt_schema`, which raises
`UnknownReceiptVersion`. Step 6 is the published schema's members and constants.
Step 7 is `_digest`, and step 8 is `deployment_receipt` over the stored evidence.
The backend makes no step-9 or step-10 check: it holds no keyring, and how old is
too old is the reader's policy. The conformance tests hold the verifier to the
backend's answer on every vector where the backend has one, and to the ground truth
(which key signed which bytes, and when the backend's clock said they were issued)
where the only defect is the signature or the age.

## 8. Versions

### 8.1 A version the reader does not know

A reader refuses it, naming it, and never reads it as the nearest version it does
know. The backend does the same when asked to describe one
(`UnknownReceiptVersion`, `assurance/receipt.py:829-854`): "a wrong schema is worse
than no schema". Note that the backend's `receipt_schema(None)` returns the
current schema, so a caller passing `receipt.get("receipt_version")` for a receipt
with no version gets 4.1's. The verifier refuses such a receipt instead.

### 8.2 History

| Version | Introduced | Added | Digest of the same state against the version before |
|---|---|---|---|
| `mythos.assurance.receipt/1.0` | #27, `673a40b`, 17 Sep 2026 | `receipt_version`, `system`, `result`, `policy`, `evidence`, `assessments` | (first) |
| `mythos.assurance.receipt/1.1` | #42, `5ed69be`, 18 Sep 2026 | `policy_version` | differs |
| `mythos.assurance.receipt/2.0` | #56, `fdf77bf`, 22 Sep 2026 | `served_route`, `coverage` | differs |
| `mythos.assurance.receipt/3.0` | #70, `99bc3d9`, 22 Sep 2026 | `coverage.checks_gap_fingerprint`, `coverage.checks_reported_at` | differs |
| `mythos.assurance.receipt/3.1` | #87, `58083c1`, 23 Sep 2026 | `signed`, `signature`, `unsigned_reason`, outside the digest | differs in `receipt_version` and `policy_version` only |
| `mythos.assurance.receipt/4.0` | #105, `7985460`, 26 Sep 2026 | `chains` | differs |
| `mythos.assurance.receipt/4.1` | the commit that added this file, 29 Sep 2026 | `issued_at`, in the signed form only, outside the digest | differs in `receipt_version` only |

`algorithm`, `digest` and `computed_at` are in every version, outside the digest.
The digest rule (5.2) and the canonical form (5.1) have not changed since 1.0.

**The digest across a MINOR step.** `receipt_version` is itself hashed (5.2), so
every version step changes the digest of an unchanged state, MINOR ones included.
A MINOR step adds, removes or retypes no hashed member: the digest a 4.1 receipt
gives a state is the one a 4.0 receipt gives it with `receipt_version` changed.
v4.0.md's history said "identical" for 3.1, which was true of neither of the two
values that moved then. `receipt_version` was one. `policy_version` was the other:
the assurance policy pin then embedded the version the receipt is stamped with, so
3.1 moved the pin, and with it every claim bound to it. From 4.1 the pin embeds the
version that introduced the hashed content instead (`HASHED_CONTENT_VERSION`,
`assurance/receipt.py:229-236`; `assurance/policy.py:107`), which a MINOR step
leaves where it is. 4.1 left every pin, and so every `policy_version`, unchanged.

### 8.3 Reading an older version

Every version is read by the same rules: the canonical form of 5.1, and the digest
of 5.2 over the members that version hashes.

| Version | Hashed members | Full form adds | Signed form adds |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.1 | `receipt_version`, `policy_version`, `system`, `result`, `policy`, `evidence`, `assessments` | `algorithm`, `digest`, `computed_at` | `algorithm`, `digest` |
| 2.0, 3.0 | 1.1's, plus `served_route`, `coverage` | `algorithm`, `digest`, `computed_at` | `algorithm`, `digest` |
| 3.1 | as 3.0 | `algorithm`, `digest`, `computed_at`, `signed`, `signature`, `unsigned_reason` | `algorithm`, `digest` |
| 4.0 | 3.1's, plus `chains` | as 3.1 | `algorithm`, `digest` |
| 4.1 | as 4.0 | as 3.1 | `algorithm`, `digest`, `issued_at` |

The backend publishes each version's schema: `receipt_schema(version)` for 1.1,
2.0, 3.0, 3.1, 4.0 and 4.1. The verifier reads the same six, and the conformance
tests check that its member tables are the backend's schemas, and that the versions
it expects an `issued_at` from are exactly the ones whose schema defines one.
Receipts were first signed under 3.1 (#96), and first signed with a time under 4.1.

Two versions are not cleanly readable (section 10): 1.0, which no registry
describes, and 2.0, which has two shapes of `coverage`.

### 8.4 The rule for the next version

1. A change to hashed content bumps the MAJOR number. That covers a member added,
   removed, renamed or retyped anywhere inside `H`, at any depth.
2. A change only to members outside the digest bumps the MINOR number (3.0 to 3.1,
   4.0 to 4.1). No hashed member changes; the digest of an unchanged state still
   moves, by the version string alone (8.2).
3. A version string names one shape. It is never reused for a different one, as
   2.0 was.
4. Each version gets its own file, `docs/receipt-spec/v<major>.<minor>.md`. Older
   files stay, and `docs/receipt-spec/README.md` lists them all. The conformance
   tests fail while `RECEIPT_VERSION` has no file.
5. The backend keeps every older version describable (`_ADDED_IN`, `receipt_schema`),
   and the verifier's `HASHED` table gains the new version. The conformance tests
   fail if the verifier and the backend's schemas disagree about any version.
6. The envelope (`envelope_version` 1, payload type `T`) is versioned separately
   from the receipt. A receipt version change does not change it.
7. The assurance policy pins `HASHED_CONTENT_VERSION`, not `RECEIPT_VERSION`. A
   MAJOR step moves it, and so moves the pin; a MINOR step moves neither.

## 9. The offline verifier

`tools/verify_receipt.py` is written from this specification, not from the
backend's code, and imports nothing from this repository. It needs the Python
standard library and, to check a signature, `cryptography` (Ed25519), the one crypto
library the backend already depends on. CI runs it on Python 3.11. Copy it anywhere.

```
python tools/verify_receipt.py RECEIPT.json --keyring KEYRING.json [--evidence EVIDENCE.json] [--max-age SECONDS]
python tools/verify_receipt.py RECEIPT.json --public-key KEY.b64 [--evidence EVIDENCE.json] [--max-age SECONDS]
python tools/verify_receipt.py RECEIPT.json      # the unsigned copy: checked, then refused as unsigned
```

- `RECEIPT.json` is the signed route's answer, the unsigned copy, or an envelope on
  its own, saved as served.
- `--keyring` is the engine's published keyring (6.4). Entries are checked when it
  is loaded: an id that is not its key's digest, a key enrolled twice or an unknown
  status make the keyring unusable.
- `--public-key` is a file holding one base64 Ed25519 public key. It is treated as
  an `active` key under its derived id, so it carries no revocation: use the
  keyring where you can.
- `--evidence` is the evidence file of 5.3.
- `--max-age SECONDS` is your freshness policy (step 10): a receipt issued longer
  ago than that, by its signed `issued_at` and this machine's clock, is refused
  `stale`, and one that carries no signed time (4.0 and older) is refused
  `no_signed_time`. It is a whole number of seconds, 0 or more.

Output. The first line is `VERIFIED` or `REFUSED <reason>`, with `<reason>` from the
table in section 7, and the lines after it say why. A verified receipt's summary
names the version, system, decision, digest, signer and status, the issue time, and
whether the evidence root was re-derived. The issue time is shown as:

- `issued at <issued_at> by the issuer's clock, N s before this machine's clock` for
  a 4.1 receipt;
- `issued at <issued_at> by the issuer's clock, N s AHEAD of this machine's clock:
  one of the two clocks is wrong` when it is later than this machine's clock. It is
  never shown as an age of 0;
- `issued at not signed (4.0): a 4.0 receipt carries no signing time` for a 4.0
  receipt (and the same for an older one, with its version). Never a time.

| Exit status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 0 | Verified. |
| 1 | Refused; the first line names the reason. |
| 2 | Could not run: bad arguments (a `--max-age` that is not a whole number of seconds, 0 or more, among them), an unreadable file, a keyring or evidence file not in the specified form, or a signed receipt with no key given. The message is on standard error and starts `CANNOT RUN:`, or is argparse's own usage error for an argument it cannot parse. |

The conformance vectors are the gate. Beside them,
`tests/test_a_signed_receipt_says_when_it_was_issued.py` signs through the real
route with a stand-in that signs as athena-engine's `wrap` does, and checks that
the time is in the signed bytes, that an edited time fails the signature, that two
signings of one state carry one digest, that a 4.0 receipt still verifies, and that
the policy pin did not move.

## 10. Known limitations of 4.1

Said here so nothing in a receipt reads as more than it is:

1. **The signed time is the issuer's clock.** `issued_at` is athena-backend's clock
   when it had the receipt signed. Nothing checks that clock, and it is not proof
   of when the state held. A backend clock set ahead would make its receipts look
   fresher for as long as it is ahead; the verifier shows a time ahead of its own
   clock as that, and does not refuse it.
2. **A 4.0 or older signed receipt carries no time**, and verifies for ever while
   its key is not revoked. A reader with a freshness policy refuses it
   (`no_signed_time`); a reader without one cannot tell its age.
3. **1.0 receipts cannot be read.** #27 served `mythos.assurance.receipt/1.0`; #42
   replaced it, and no registry has described it since. Readers refuse it as
   `unknown_version`: a refusal, never a misreading. Its digest follows the same
   rule (5.2) over the six members it carries.
4. **2.0 has two shapes.** #56 served a five-member `coverage`. #67, the same day
   and still as 2.0, added `checks_reported`, `checks_total`, `checks_performed` and
   `checks_complete`. Both digest correctly by 5.2, and this verifier reads both,
   since it does not check nested members. But the published 2.0 schema requires
   the nine-member form, so a first-shape 2.0 receipt fails the schema published to
   read it.
5. **The fingerprint comes only from the incident pack.** The finding list serves
   the uuid and the evidence rows of each leaf but not the fingerprint. The module
   docstring says an auditor can recompute a finding's digest from "classification,
   source and content hash, all of which the API already returns"
   (`assurance/receipt.py:6-9`); the fingerprint is needed too (5.3).
6. **Two receipts say nothing about signatures.** The bare `receipt` route and each
   finding's `receipt` carry a prominent SHA-256 `digest` and no `signed` field.
   That is the reading 3.1 fixed for the full receipt.
7. **The keyring pointer names an engine path.** The signed route's `verify` text
   says "GET /api/assurance/keyring". That is the engine's route; the backend does
   not serve it. The client's `assurance_keyring()`, whose docstring says
   "Proxied", is called by no route.
8. **The decision has seven values**, while the schema's description of `result`
   says "six-state".
9. **The signed route's docstring still says the same state signs to the same
   envelope** (`assurance/views.py:725-731`). From 4.1 it signs to the same digest
   in an envelope that differs by its `issued_at`; section 6.1 is what the code
   does.
