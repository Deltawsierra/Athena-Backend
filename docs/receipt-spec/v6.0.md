## 1. What a receipt attests, and what it does not

In the module's own words (`assurance/receipt.py:11-19`):

> - it attests **integrity and provenance** — *this is the evidence that was
>   recorded, unaltered*;
> - it does **not** attest that the conclusion is *true*. How strongly a finding
>   is actually known is the evidence *class* (`technically_verified` down to
>   `vendor_asserted`), a separate axis this receipt never inflates. A receipt
>   over a `vendor_asserted` claim proves the claim was recorded faithfully, not
>   that the vendor is right.

and (`assurance/receipt.py:77-80`): "the receipt attests **integrity and
provenance** — *this is the assurance state that was recorded, unaltered* — and
never that the conclusions are true or the system is secure."

So, precisely:

- **A verified signature** establishes that this is the assurance state
  athena-backend recorded, unaltered since the holder of the named key signed it,
  and that the key was trusted to sign (`active`) or had been rotated out without
  being revoked (`retired`).
- **A matching digest on an unsigned copy** establishes only that the copy is
  internally consistent. A digest is a checksum anyone can recompute; it says
  nothing about who produced the receipt.
- **Neither establishes that the assessment is correct or the system safe.** A
  `needs_more_evidence` decision, or an assessment resting on `vendor_asserted`
  evidence, is carried at its true strength. A valid signature over a weak claim
  is a valid signature over a weak claim.
- **A signed receipt says when it was issued, from 4.1 on, and no more than that.** Its
  signed form carries `issued_at` (section 4.1): athena-backend's clock at the
  moment it handed the receipt to the engine to be signed, inside the signature
  and outside `digest`. A time edited after signing fails the signature. What it
  does not establish is that the clock was right, or when the state it describes
  came to hold or stopped holding: it is the issuer's clock, not a trusted
  timestamp. Receipts are signed on read, so `issued_at` is the time of the read
  that produced this copy, not the time of any assessment. A signed `ready` from
  before a regression still verifies for ever (a retired key goes on verifying
  what it signed), but from 4.1 it says how old it is, and a reader with a
  freshness policy refuses it. How old is too old is the reader's to decide; the
  offline verifier's `--max-age` applies one (sections 7 and 9).
- **A 4.0 or older signed receipt carries no time.** Its signed form left out
  `computed_at` and put nothing in its place, and the envelope carries no time, so
  the same assurance state signed to the same bytes on any day. The verifier says
  it is `not signed (4.0)` and never shows a time for it; under `--max-age` it
  refuses it (`no_signed_time`). The times inside the digest, such as
  `coverage.checks_reported_at`, are when a measurement was reported, not when a
  receipt was issued.
- **A receipt is a reduction.** It carries counts and digests, not the rows behind
  them: no finding list, no route detail, no named coverage gaps, no workflow
  names. Those are retrievable from the deployment; the receipt binds digests of
  them (section 5.4).
- **From 5.0 a receipt says which authority produced each consequential effect,
  and which hops of it nothing proves** (section 4.10). Each authority chain in
  force is listed by its digest, hop by hop, with the verdict the backend reached
  when it computed the receipt: `proven`, `unproven` or `broken`, and the codes of
  what proves the hop or holds it. The verdicts are the backend's reading of its own
  record at that moment -- its graph, its approvals, its coverage manifest and the
  signed outcomes the chain cites -- and a verified receipt attests that this is the
  reading that was recorded, not that the authority it describes was sound. Nor
  does a `proven` hop say more than its codes do: an Action Gate permit proves the
  gate let the action through, not that the effect happened. No node is named.
- **From 6.0 a receipt says which consequential effects no authority chain names**
  (section 4.11). Every tool an approved workflow's approval binds is one effect of
  that workflow, and its class is read off the tool's declared contract:
  `consequential` (`write`, `destructive`, or annotated destructive), `read_only`
  (`read`), or `unknown` (undeclared, a label outside that vocabulary, or a tool no
  longer registered). A consequential effect no chain in force names is `missing`; an
  unknown one is `unknown`. Both read unproven: a deployment that recorded no chain no
  longer reads like one whose chains all hold. The class is what the tool's
  registration declares, which a receipt attests was declared, not that it is true.
  No workflow or tool is named.

## 2. Where receipts are served

Every route below is a read under `/api/assurance/`, open to any authenticated
operator (`IsAuthenticated`), computed on read and never stored.

| Route | Serves | Signed |
|---|---|---|
| `GET deployments/<uuid>/assurance-receipt/` (`assurance/views.py:798-822`) | The receipt, full form (section 4). | No, and it says so: `signed: false`, `signature: null`, `unsigned_reason`. |
| `GET deployments/<uuid>/signed-assurance-receipt/` (`assurance/views.py:824-965`) | The signed form of the same receipt, stamped with the time it was issued, beside a DSSE envelope over it, or the reason it could not be signed (section 6.6). | Yes, when the engine signs. |
| `GET deployments/<uuid>/receipt/` (`assurance/views.py:788-796`) | The bare evidence root: `algorithm`, `digest` (= `evidence.root`), `finding_count`, `computed_at`. | No, and it carries no field that says so (section 10). |
| `GET findings/`, `GET findings/<uuid>/` | Each finding's `receipt`: `algorithm`, `digest` (the leaf of section 5.3), `evidence_count`, `computed_at`. | No. |
| `GET findings/<uuid>/incident-pack/` | `identity.finding.uuid`, `identity.finding.fingerprint`, `evidence.rows` and the finding's `receipt`: everything the leaf covers (section 5.3). | No (the pack's own signing is out of scope here). |
| `GET claims/` and a deployment's `assurance-claims/` | `receipt_digest`: the top-level `digest` of the state a claim was derived from (`assurance/claims.py:1194`). | No. |

The engine serves the two routes the signature needs. `POST
/api/assurance/receipt/sign` is operator-only, and the backend calls it
(`ai_engine/services/cyberengine_client.py:527-545`). `GET /api/assurance/keyring`
is unauthenticated, and is where the engine publishes its public keyring (section
6.4). The backend does not serve the keyring.

**Who signs what.** The engine signs the document the backend hands it and builds
none of its own. Its route takes `{"receipt": P}` (athena-engine
`api/server.py:453-465`) and passes `P` to `wrap` (`api/server.py:2744-2770`), which
serialises it (section 6.2) and signs that; it adds no member and no time. So the
backend decides every byte of the signed form, `issued_at` included, and the
engine's clock appears nowhere in a receipt.

## 3. Conventions

- A receipt is JSON (RFC 8259). No object in it names a member twice.
- Every number in a receipt is an integer. None carries a fraction or an exponent.
- A digest is 64 lowercase hexadecimal characters of SHA-256.
- `issued_at` is an RFC 3339 date-time in exactly one form:
  `YYYY-MM-DDTHH:MM:SSZ`, UTC, whole seconds. Every other date-time in a receipt
  (`computed_at`, `coverage.checks_reported_at`) is ISO 8601 as Python's
  `isoformat()` writes it.
- **Not known is said, never defaulted into a pass.** Each member that can be
  unknown has one explicit way of saying so, and none of them is `0`, `true` or an
  empty list standing in for "fine":

| Member | How "not known" or "not declared" is said |
|---|---|
| `result.decision` | `null`: no decision has been computed. Never read as `ready`. |
| `policy` | `{"declared": false}` and nothing else: no data boundary was ever approved, and none is invented. |
| `served_route.unknown_fields` | Names every route field never observed. A route field nothing observed is the string `unknown`, inside the route detail the fingerprint covers. |
| `served_route.complete` | `false` when there are no routes: nothing served is not everything observed. |
| `coverage.verdict` | `undeclared`: with no declared architecture there is no promise to be short of. Its own answer, not a weak `complete`. |
| `coverage.checks_reported` | `false`: no engine said which checks it ran. Then `checks_total`, `checks_performed`, `checks_complete`, `checks_gap_fingerprint` and `checks_reported_at` are all `null`, never `0` or `false`. |
| `chains.approved_set_recorded` | `false`: nobody recorded the approved workflow set. Then `workflows_expected` is `null`. |
| `chains.rule_decision` | `null`: no chain was assessed. Never read as `ready`. |
| `chains.census` and the other censuses | Every key of the vocabulary is present, zeros included: a count that appeared only when non-zero would read like a zero when missing. |
| `signed`, `signature`, `unsigned_reason` | `false`, `null` and a sentence: this copy is unsigned, said in the payload. |
| `issued_at` | Always present in a 4.1, 5.0 or 6.0 signed form, never unknown there. A 4.0 or older signed form has no `issued_at` at all: its time is not known, and no reader supplies one. |
| `authority_chains.chains` | `[]` with `authority_chains.standing` `0`: no authority chain was recorded. Then nothing about the authority behind an effect was assessed, which is not the same as every chain proven. |
| `authority_chains.verdict_census`, `authority_chains.basis_census` | Every verdict and every basis is present, zeros included. |
| `consequential_effects.effects` | `[]` with every count in `consequential_effects.status_census` `0`: no approval binds a tool, so no effect is named. That is not the same as every effect covered. |
| `consequential_effects.status_census` | Every status is present, zeros included. |

## 4. The receipt, member by member

The receipt has two forms. The **full form** is what `assurance-receipt` serves.
The **signed form** is the full form without `computed_at`, `signed`, `signature`
and `unsigned_reason`, and with `issued_at`: the envelope's payload, and the
`receipt` served beside it (section 6.1). Every member listed is required in the
form it belongs to. The only optional members in a 6.0 receipt are the three inside
`policy` that are present exactly when `policy.declared` is `true`.

### 4.1 Top level

| Member | Type | In `digest` | In the signature | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `receipt_version` | string | yes | yes | `mythos.assurance.receipt/6.0`. A reader keys on it (section 8). |
| `policy_version` | string | yes | yes | The pinned assurance-policy version the decision was made under: `mythos.assurance.policy/1.0+` and the first 12 hex characters of a digest over the governing rules (`assurance/policy.py:271-282`). The same rules always give the same pin. |
| `system` | object | yes | yes | The system under assurance (4.2). |
| `result` | object | yes | yes | The deployment decision (4.3). |
| `policy` | object | yes | yes | The declared data boundary (4.4). |
| `evidence` | object | yes | yes | The evidence root (4.5, 5.3). |
| `assessments` | object | yes | yes | One digest per computed assessment (4.6). |
| `served_route` | object | yes | yes | What actually ran (4.7). |
| `coverage` | object | yes | yes | What was and was not assessed (4.8). |
| `chains` | object | yes | yes | How the approved workflow chains composed into the decision (4.9). |
| `authority_chains` | object | yes | yes | Which authority chain produced each consequential effect, hop by hop, and each hop's verdict (4.10). New in 5.0. |
| `consequential_effects` | object | yes | yes | Every effect the approvals cover, its class, and whether a chain in force names it (4.11). New in 6.0. |

Signed form only (`SIGNED_FORM_ONLY`, `assurance/receipt.py:179`; stamped by `signable_receipt`, `assurance/receipt.py:1627-1656`). The issuer's clock, not proof of when the state held (section 1). New in 4.1. |

every census carries every key of its vocabulary, zeros included
(`assurance/receipt.py:1170-1203`).

| Member | Type | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `chains.approved_set_recorded` | boolean | Whether anyone recorded the approved workflow set. |
| `chains.workflows_expected` | integer or null | Approved workflows. `null` when the set was not recorded. |
| `chains.workflows_assessed` | integer | Workflows with a standing outcome, counting an approved one nothing reported on as `not_demonstrated`. |
| `chains.workflows_unreported` | integer | Approved workflows no outcome was recorded for. |
| `chains.workflows_unapproved` | integer | Workflows an outcome arrived for that are not approved. |
| `chains.rule_decision` | string or null | What the composition rule alone reaches, before findings, claims and coverage are combined into `result` (a value from 4.3). `null` when no chain was assessed. |
| `chains.census` | object of integers | Standing outcomes by status: `held`, `violated`, `not_demonstrated`, `incomplete`. |
| `chains.basis_census` | object of integers | By basis: `demonstrated` (a key this deployment trusts signed it), `attested` (someone typed it in), `unknown` (nobody said). |
| `chains.workflows_unexercised` | integer | Standing outcomes whose status claims an exercise while their basis does not say one happened. |
| `chains.evidence_census` | object of integers | By kind of evidence: `authorization_check`, `scan`, `observed_effect`, `unclassified`, `attested`, `unknown`. A held resting on an authorization check shows the action was authorized, not that the effect happened within that authority. |
| `chains.route_census` | object of integers | By the served route the outcome was taken against: `current`, `moved`, `unrecorded`. |

### 4.10 `authority_chains`

Every authority chain in force, hop by hop, with each hop's verdict (`assurance/authority_chain.py`
states the rule; `assurance/authority_chain_records.py` loads its inputs and builds this block,
`receipt_reference`). New in 5.0.

An **authority chain** is the ordered list of hops one consequential effect was produced through,
each hop `(from, relation, to)`, recorded by `POST /api/assurance/deployments/<uuid>/authority-chains/`
and served back, whole and named, by `GET` on the same route. A chain is **in force** when it is the
newest recorded for its workflow and effect; an older one for the same effect is superseded, kept and
counted, and not listed here.

| Member | Type | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `authority_chains.recorded` | integer | Chains ever recorded for the deployment, superseded ones included. |
| `authority_chains.standing` | integer | Chains in force: the newest per workflow and effect. |
| `authority_chains.superseded` | integer | Chains a newer one for the same effect replaced. `recorded` is `standing` plus `superseded`. |
| `authority_chains.verdict_census` | object of integers | Chains in force by verdict: `proven`, `unproven`, `broken`, every one present, zeros included. They sum to `standing`. |
| `authority_chains.basis_census` | object of integers | Chains in force by what they rest on: `demonstrated` (an engine signed the chain, with an envelope that verifies now against the backend's outcome keyring and names the chain's digest as its evidence digest) or `attested` (a person recorded it). |
| `authority_chains.chains` | array of objects | The chains in force, ordered by `digest`, at most 50. |
| `authority_chains.not_shown` | integer | Chains in force past the 50 listed. The list's length plus this is `standing`. |

Each element of `authority_chains.chains` has exactly these four members:

| Member | Type | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `digest` | string | `sha256:` and 64 lowercase hex: SHA-256 over the chain document `{"schema": "mythos.authority-chain/v1", "deployment", "workflow", "hops", "gate_outcome_id", "effect_outcome_id"}`, serialised with sorted keys, no whitespace and raw UTF-8 (mythos-core's outcome form, `evidence_digest_of`; not 5.1's). The authority-chains route serves each chain with this digest beside it, so a reader holding the receipt can fetch the chain it names. |
| `basis` | string | `demonstrated` or `attested`, as in `authority_chains.basis_census`. |
| `verdict` | string | The worst verdict among its hops: `broken` over `unproven` over `proven`. |
| `hops` | array of objects | Its hops, in chain order. Never empty. |

Each hop has exactly these five members:

| Member | Type | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `relation` | string | One of `authenticated_as`, `delegates_to`, `under_policy`, `invokes`, `through_identity`, `performs`, `produces`. |
| `from_kind`, `to_kind` | string | The kinds the hop joins, one of `action`, `agent`, `effect`, `mcp_server`, `person`, `policy`, `service_account`, `skill`, `tool`, `user`. |
| `verdict` | string | `proven`, `unproven` or `broken` (below). |
| `readings` | array of strings | For a `proven` hop, the code of every piece of record that proves it; otherwise the code of every reason it is not proven, the deciding one first. Codes below. |

**The relations, and the kinds each joins:**

| Relation | From | To |
|---|---|---|
| `authenticated_as` | `person` | `user` |
| `delegates_to` | `person`, `user`, `agent` | `agent` |
| `under_policy` | `agent` | `policy` |
| `invokes` | `policy`, `agent` | `tool`, `mcp_server`, `skill` |
| `through_identity` | `tool`, `mcp_server`, `skill` | `service_account` |
| `performs` | `service_account`, `agent` | `action` |
| `produces` | `action` | `effect` |

**The verdicts.** `proven`: every check that applies to the hop passed, and something on record
supports it. `unproven`: nothing contradicts the hop, and something it needs is missing, unverified,
shadow, superseded or not in force. `broken`: the record contradicts it. A hop no check speaks for,
and a relation this platform holds no record of, is `unproven`, never `proven`. So is `produces` unless
the chain cites an `observed_effect` outcome bound to it: one Achilles signed with its observed-effect
key (observer `achilles-effect`) when the dispatch that carried the action out saw its provider complete
it, whose evidence document names the chain's tool and the gate decision it cites, and that no other chain
in force cites (section 10). `authenticated_as` is proven only by a signed `authentication` record (the
sign-in collector's key, observer `mythos-signin-collector`) naming the hop's person and principal, whose
sign-in falls inside the chain's window -- at most 24 hours before the effect's instant, the cited observed
effect's, and not after it -- in a session still open at the effect. `delegates_to` is proven only by a
signed `delegation` record (the grant collector's key, observer `mythos-grant-collector`) naming the hop's
principal and agent, whose scope covers every action the chain performs after it, whose window holds the
effect's instant, and which was not revoked as of the effect: a revocation after the effect does not
unprove it, one before (or at) it does (`assurance/identity_evidence.py`).
**Read as of dispatch** (part 5 of the 7 Oct decision). `under_policy` and `invokes` are proven against
the state the effect's dispatch ran under, never the record as it stands when the receipt is computed.
Achilles signs that state into the observed effect (`mythos.observed-effect/v2`, its `dispatch` block):
from its service, the instant the dispatch left, its authority epoch at the spend and the permit's, and
its operator policy at the spend and the permit's; as presented, the approval digest, the tool contract
digests, the served route and the sign-in assertion and grant digests the dispatch was made under. The
backend proves those against the history it keeps -- every approval version (`ApprovalVersion`,
append-only), every tool contract (`ToolContract`, append-only), and the route serving at the dispatch
instant, bound when the effect is recorded. `under_policy` is proven when the chain's version is the one
signed, the gate ran in the running epoch the permit was issued under and under the permit's operator
policy, and that version was in force at the dispatch instant; `invokes` when the approval in force then
names the tool under the contract in force then, the one signed, and the route then is on record (and is
the one signed, if one was). So a re-approval, a contract change or a route move after the effect does not
unprove it, and an effect dispatched under an approval already superseded reads unproven even once that
approval is restored. A dispatch whose state nothing records -- no observed effect, a v1 one, nothing
presented, no history by then -- reads `dispatch_state_unrecorded`. The dispatch instant bounds the
effect's (at most 60 s before it, never after), and when the gate saw a sign-in assertion or a grant, only
the records of those prove the person hops.
**The graph's edges are read as of dispatch too** (the follow-up to part 5). `through_identity`,
`performs` and the reach half of `invokes` read the graph: an agent's declared identity and the identity
references discovery could not place, its declared tools and the tool references it could not place,
each principal's effective reach, inferred edges, which components declare their permissions and which
permissions. The backend keeps an append-only history of when each piece of that state came into force and
went out of it, as the platform noticed (`AuthorityEdgeVersion`, `assurance/edge_history.py`) -- the state
that contradicts a hop as well as the edge that proves it -- and each hop reaches, at the dispatch instant,
the verdict the graph as it stood then implied:
- `through_identity`: an identity reference unplaced then, `unproven` (`identity_unresolved`); this account
  then, `proven` (`identity_in_force_at_dispatch`); another account then and not this one, `broken`
  (`acts_as_another`); no identity then, `unproven` (`identity_not_in_force_at_dispatch`).
- the reach: a reference to the tool unplaced then, `unproven` (`dangling_edge`); a declared `invokes` edge or
  effective access then, `proven` (`reach_in_force_at_dispatch`); only an inferred edge, or tool references
  unplaced then, `unproven` (`reach_not_in_force_at_dispatch`); none of that, over a graph fully resolved from
  the agent then, `broken` (`unreachable`).
- `performs`: the identity, the tool it acts through or a tool it reached then declared the permission then,
  `proven` (`action_in_force_at_dispatch`); every one of them declared its permissions then and none this one,
  `broken` (`permission_not_declared`); otherwise `unproven` (`action_not_in_force_at_dispatch`). And a tool of
  the approval in force then was approved, under the contract it was approved under, with the permission
  (`within_approval_at_dispatch`; outside it, `broken`, `outside_approval`).

Each proving reading cites the history row that put the edge in force. So an edge changed after the effect
does not unprove it, and an effect made through an edge removed (or contradicted) before its dispatch reads
`unproven` (or `broken`) even once the edge is restored. A contradiction is read only of components that
are, as the chain names them now, the rows the history names (the history keeps each component's
existence and naming): one re-created, renamed or deleted since the dispatch reads `unproven`
(`*_not_in_force_at_dispatch`), never `broken`.

A chain that records **no dispatch** -- no observed effect, a v1 one, or one whose document no longer
matches its signed digest -- has no older graph to be read against. Its graph hops read
`dispatch_state_unrecorded`, and the graph as it stands never proves them; but where the graph as it stands
contradicts a hop as the live rule did, the hop is `broken`, with the same code, its detail saying it was
read from the graph as it stands because no dispatch is recorded: `acts_as_another`, `unreachable`,
`permission_not_declared`, and `outside_approval` against the approval as it stands (its latest recorded
version). A misconfiguration present now is broken whether or not anything was dispatched through it. A
chain that records a dispatch the edge history does not cover (one before the deployment's history began)
reads `dispatch_state_unrecorded` only: a chain that records a dispatch is read as of it alone, so no change
after the dispatch unproves or breaks it.
A chain with a `broken` hop holds the decision at `needs_remediation`, and one with an `unproven` hop
at `needs_more_evidence` (`CLAIM_CAPS`, `assurance/decision.py`).

### 4.11 `consequential_effects`

Every effect an approved workflow has through a tool its approval binds, its class, and whether an
authority chain in force names it (`assurance/consequential.py` states the rule;
`assurance/authority_chain_records.py` loads its inputs and builds this block, `effects_reference`).
New in 6.0.

An **effect** here is one approved workflow acting through one tool it can reach. That is every tool
its approval binds (the `tools` an approved workflow names, bound to their contracts), and every
registered tool an approval does not bind: the record holds no edge from a workflow to a tool, so
nothing says an approved workflow does NOT reach a registered tool. An approval that binds no tools
reaches every registered tool (code `approval_binds_no_tools`). A registered tool no approval binds,
when every approval binds some, is one effect of no named workflow (`tool_not_bound_to_approval`).
Neither is ever `covered`: a chain cannot show an effect is within an approval that does not name
its tool. Not binding a tool therefore never reads better than binding it. Its **class** is read off the tool's
registration as it stands when the receipt is computed -- its declared `effect_class` and MCP
`annotations`:

| Class | When |
|---|---|
| `consequential` | the declared effect class is `write` or `destructive`, or the declared annotations set `destructiveHint` to `true`. An annotation only ever raises a tool to consequential. Where two declarations of the tool disagree, the worst of them. |
| `read_only` | the declared effect class is `read`, and nothing declared says the tool is destructive. |
| `unknown` | anything else: no effect class declared, a label outside that vocabulary, or the tool no longer registered. Never read as `read_only`. |

Its **status**:

| Status | When |
|---|---|
| `covered` | consequential, and an authority chain in force for the same workflow names the tool in an `invokes` hop -- by its identifier, or by a reference that resolves to exactly that registration. The chain's own verdict (4.10) decides from there. |
| `missing` | consequential, and no chain in force names it. Holds the decision at `needs_more_evidence` (`authority_chain_missing`), as an unproven chain does. |
| `unknown` | the class is unknown. Holds the decision at `needs_more_evidence` (`effect_class_unknown`) whatever chain names it: a chain proves who produced an effect, not what kind of effect it is. |
| `not_required` | read-only: no chain is needed. |

| Member | Type | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `consequential_effects.status_census` | object of integers | Effects by status: `covered`, `missing`, `unknown`, `not_required`, every one present, zeros included. |
| `consequential_effects.effects` | array of objects | The effects, ordered by `digest`, at most 50. |

## 5. Canonical form and digests

### 5.1 The digest's canonical form

Every digest in a receipt is `hex(SHA-256(C(v)))`, lowercase, where `C(v)` is these
bytes, exactly (`_digest`, `assurance/receipt.py:284-289`, which is
`json.dumps(v, sort_keys=True, separators=(",", ":"))` encoded):

- **Objects**: `{`, members sorted by name (by Unicode code point; every name in a
  receipt is ASCII), each `"name":value`, separated by `,`, then `}`. No
  whitespace anywhere.
- **Arrays**: `[`, elements in their own order, separated by `,`, then `]`. Array
  order is content.
- **Strings**: `"` ... `"`. Every character outside U+0020..U+007E is escaped as
  `\uXXXX` with lowercase hex, and a character beyond U+FFFF as the two escapes of
  its UTF-16 surrogate pair. The exceptions are U+0008 `\b`, U+0009 `\t`,
  U+000A `\n`, U+000C `\f` and U+000D `\r`. `"` is `\"` and `\` is `\\`. U+007F is
  escaped (`\u007f`); `/` is not. The bytes are therefore pure ASCII.
- **Integers** in decimal: no leading zero, no `+`, `-` for negatives. **`true`**,
  **`false`**, **`null`** as written.

Example: `C({"b": 1, "a": "Zü😀"})` is the 33 bytes
`{"a":"Z\u00fc\ud83d\ude00","b":1}`.

The backend's `_digest` also passes `default=str`. It never applies to a receipt,
because every value in one is a JSON string, integer, boolean, null, array or
object. The conformance tests recompute every digest from the bytes the routes
serve.

What does not change a digest is how the JSON you hold is laid out: member order
and whitespace. What does change it is any value, any member name, array order and
any number.

### 5.2 The top-level digest

`digest = hex(SHA-256(C(H)))`, where `H` is the receipt with only these members:
`receipt_version`, `policy_version`, `system`, `result`, `policy`, `evidence`,
`assessments`, `served_route`, `coverage`, `chains`, `authority_chains`,
`consequential_effects`. That is every
member except `algorithm`, `digest`, `issued_at`, `computed_at`, `signed`, `signature` and
`unsigned_reason`. The rule is the same in every version: each has hashed exactly
the members it carries other than those seven (section 8).

So no clock enters the digest: two signings of one state carry one `digest`, the
same one the unsigned copy carries.

### 5.3 The evidence root

For a deployment `D` with findings `F1 ... Fn`:

1. For each finding `F`, take its evidence as rows `[classification, source,
   content_hash]`, with an empty value as `""`, and sort the rows: element by
   element, by code point (`assurance/receipt.py:319-328`).
2. `leaf(F) = hex(SHA-256(C({"evidence": rows, "fingerprint": F.fingerprint,
   "uuid": F.uuid})))` (`finding_receipt`, `assurance/receipt.py:331-347`).
3. `evidence.root = hex(SHA-256(C({"deployment": D.uuid, "findings": sorted leaf
   digests})))`, and `evidence.finding_count = n` (`deployment_receipt`,
   `assurance/receipt.py:350-366`). With no findings the root is still taken, over
   `"findings": []`, so it is still bound to the deployment.

There is no domain-separation prefix. The two levels are told apart by the member
names of the object each hashes (`evidence`, `fingerprint`, `uuid` against
`deployment`, `findings`), so a leaf's canonical bytes can never be a root's. A leaf
enters the root as its 64-character hex string.

**Getting the evidence.** List the deployment's findings with `GET
/api/assurance/findings/?deployment=<uuid>`, following every page. For each, `GET
/api/assurance/findings/<uuid>/incident-pack/` gives `identity.finding.uuid`,
`identity.finding.fingerprint` and `evidence.rows` (already sorted). Use the
incident pack: the finding list serves the evidence rows but not the fingerprint,
and the leaf covers the fingerprint. The per-finding `receipt.digest` both routes
serve is `leaf(F)`. A reader who is not an admin or analyst sees only the findings
of scans they ran or deployments they own, and a set missing one finding cannot
reproduce the root.

**The evidence file** that `tools/verify_receipt.py --evidence` reads:

```json
{
  "deployment": "<system.uuid>",
  "findings": [
    {"uuid": "<finding uuid>", "fingerprint": "<finding fingerprint>",
     "evidence": [["<classification>", "<source>", "<content_hash>"]]}
  ]
}
```

### 5.4 The other digests a receipt binds

`assessments.*`, `served_route.fingerprint` and `coverage.checks_gap_fingerprint`
are digests the backend takes, with the same canonical form, over detail the
receipt deliberately does not carry. The top-level digest and the signature bind
them. A verifier does not recompute them from the receipt, and this specification
does not define how to rebuild their inputs.

## 6. The signature

### 6.1 What is signed

The **signed form** `P` is the receipt without `computed_at`, `signed`,
`signature` and `unsigned_reason` (`NOT_SIGNED_OVER`, `assurance/receipt.py:156`),
and with `issued_at` (`SIGNED_FORM_ONLY`, `assurance/receipt.py:179`). `P` is made
by `signable_receipt` (`assurance/receipt.py:1627-1656`), which stamps `issued_at`
with the backend's clock when the signed route builds `P` and hands it to the
engine (`assurance/views.py:896`, `915`). `P` keeps `algorithm` and `digest`, so the
signature covers every hashed member, those two, and the time it was issued. The
reason for leaving the four out, in the payload's own words
(`NOT_SIGNED_OVER_REASON`, `assurance/receipt.py:161-173`):

> `computed_at` is when the unsigned copy was rendered, and nothing signs it. The
> signed copy carries its own time instead: `issued_at`, when this backend handed
> it to be signed, inside the signature and outside `digest`. So two signings of
> the SAME assurance state carry the same `digest` and differ by their `issued_at`
> alone: 'has anything changed?' is answered by comparing digests, and 'when was
> this issued?' by the signed time. `signed`, `signature` and `unsigned_reason` are
> a document's self-report about whether it is signed, which is false the moment
> it is signed -- whether an artifact carries a signature is a property of the
> envelope around it, not a claim the content can make about itself.

Two signings of one state therefore differ in `issued_at` alone, and so do their
envelopes. "Has anything changed?" is a comparison of digests, never of envelopes.

### 6.2 The payload bytes

`B = UTF-8(JSON(P))` with members sorted by name and no whitespace, and **with
non-ASCII characters raw**: only `"`, `\` and U+0000..U+001F are escaped
(athena-engine `engine/evidence/envelope.py:97-106`, `ensure_ascii=False`). This is
not the digest's form: `ü` is the two bytes `C3 BC` here and the six characters
`\u00fc` in 5.1. The signature is over `B` exactly as it arrives. A verifier never
re-serialises the payload to check a signature; it re-serialises it only to
recompute the digest.

### 6.3 Pre-authentication encoding

The bytes signed are DSSE's pre-authentication encoding (athena-engine
`engine/evidence/envelope.py:75-94`):

```
PAE = "DSSEv1" SP len(T) SP T SP len(B) SP B
```

`SP` is one space (0x20). The lengths are byte counts in ASCII decimal. `T` is
`application/vnd.mythos.assurance-receipt+json` (45 bytes), so every signed
receipt's PAE begins `DSSEv1 45 application/vnd.mythos.assurance-receipt+json `.
The type is inside the signed bytes, so a signature made over the same bytes as
another kind of document (`application/vnd.mythos.evidence-pack+json`) does not
verify as a receipt.

### 6.4 Algorithm, key identity and the keyring

- The signature is **Ed25519** (RFC 8032, pure), 64 bytes.
- A key's id is `sha256:` followed by the first 32 hex characters of SHA-256 over
  its 32-byte raw public key (athena-engine `engine/evidence/signing.py:128-134`,
  `engine/evidence/keyring.py:116`). An id is its key's digest, so one key has
  exactly one id. That is what keeps a revoked key revoked: revocation is recorded
  against the id.
- The engine publishes its **keyring** at `GET /api/assurance/keyring`. It holds
  public keys only:

```json
{"version": 1,
 "keys": {"sha256:<32 hex>": {"public_key": "<base64 of the 32 raw bytes>",
                             "status": "active", "created_at": "...",
                             "retired_at": null, "reason": null}},
 "caveat": "..."}
```

- **Statuses**: `active` signs and verifies. `retired` never signs, and verifies
  what it signed for ever, so rotating a key does not destroy published receipts.
  `revoked` verifies nothing, and every receipt signed with it is invalid.
- Fetch the keyring out of band from the receipt. A key taken from the same place
  as the artifact it checks proves only that the two agree with each other.

### 6.5 The envelope

```json
{"envelope_version": 1,
 "payloadType": "application/vnd.mythos.assurance-receipt+json",
 "payload": "<base64 of B>",
 "signatures": [{"keyid": "sha256:<32 hex>", "sig": "<base64 of the 64-byte signature>"}]}
```

Base64 is the standard alphabet with padding. The engine emits exactly one
signature (athena-engine `engine/evidence/envelope.py:120-168`). The envelope
carries no time of its own; the only signed time is `issued_at`, inside `B`.

### 6.6 The signed route's answer

Signed:

```json
{"receipt": P, "not_signed_over": {"fields": ["computed_at", "signed", "signature", "unsigned_reason"], "why": "..."},
 "signed": true, "reason": null, "envelope": E, "verify": "..."}
```

Not signed (the engine unreachable, unconfigured or refusing, or its answer not an
envelope over `P`):

```json
{"receipt": P, "not_signed_over": {...}, "signed": false, "reason": "<why, in the backend's words>", "envelope": null}
```

Only the envelope's payload is signed. `receipt` is a copy for reading, and a
verifier checks that it is the payload (step 4). `signed`, `reason`,
`not_signed_over` and `verify` are the backend's report about the envelope, and a
verifier does not rely on them. `not_signed_over.fields` names what the full form
carries and `P` does not; `issued_at`, which `P` carries and the full form does not,
is explained in its `why`. On the unsigned answer `P` still carries an `issued_at`:
the time it was handed to be signed, which nothing then signed.

### 6.7 How an unsigned receipt says so

- The full form says it in its own payload: `signed: false`, `signature: null`,
  `unsigned_reason: "<why>"`. All three are outside the digest, so the digest of a
  state is the same whether or not a signature exists, and outside the signature,
  where they would deny the signature they sit inside.
- The signed route says it beside the receipt: `signed: false`, a `reason`, and
  `envelope: null`, never an envelope with an empty signature list.
- The bare `receipt` route and the per-finding receipts are unsigned checksums with
  no field that says so (section 10).

### 6.8 What the signature does not cover

- `envelope_version` and `keyid`: outside the pre-authentication encoding, as DSSE
  defines it. `keyid` is a hint resolved against a keyring you hold, and the
  keyring's id is checked against the key's material.
- Anything outside the envelope: the route's `receipt` copy, `signed`, `reason`,
  `not_signed_over`, `verify`.
- The four members of 6.1 that the signed form leaves out. The time IS covered from
  4.1: `issued_at` is inside `B`. A 4.0 or older signed receipt covers no time.

### 6.9 What the backend checks before it serves `signed: true`

`envelope_over` (`assurance/receipt.py:1478-1609`) checks:

- the envelope is an object;
- `envelope_version` is 1 (compared with Python's `!=`, so it also accepts `true`
  and `1.0`; the verifier requires the integer 1);
- the payload type is `T`;
- there is at least one signature entry with a `keyid` and a `sig`;
- the payload is base64;
- the payload nests at most 64 levels (#111);
- the payload is JSON equal, as decoded content, to the document it sent, which
  includes the `issued_at` it stamped.

It does not check the signature: "That needs the keyring" (`assurance/receipt.py:1531-1533`).
The backend holds none. Verification is the auditor's job.

## 7. Verification algorithm

Inputs: the receipt file `A`, optionally a keyring or one public key obtained out of
band, optionally the evidence file (5.3), and optionally a maximum age in seconds:
the reader's freshness policy. The steps run in order, and the first refusal ends
verification.

1. **Read.** Decode `A` as JSON text (UTF-8, or UTF-16/32 by byte-order mark, as
   JSON readers detect it). If it nests deeper than 64 levels, refuse
   `depth_limit`. The depth is the number of arrays and objects open at the deepest
   point, with brackets inside strings not counted and a backslash in a string
   escaping the character after it. It is measured before parsing, and is the
   bound the backend puts on everything it reads from the engine
   (`MAX_JSON_DEPTH = 64`, `ai_engine/services/cyberengine_client.py:218`). If `A`
   is not JSON, names a member twice, or uses `NaN` or `Infinity`, refuse
   `malformed`.
2. **Classify.** An object with any of `envelope_version`, `payloadType`, `payload`,
   `signatures` is an envelope on its own. One with `receipt` and `envelope` is the
   signed route's answer: its `receipt` is in the signed form, and `envelope: null`
   means unsigned. One with `receipt_version` is the unsigned copy, in the full
   form. Anything else: `malformed`.
3. **Open the envelope** (when there is one). Each of these failures refuses
   `not_an_envelope`:
   - `envelope_version` is not the integer 1;
   - `payloadType` is not exactly `T`;
   - `signatures` is not a non-empty array of objects, each with a non-empty
     string `keyid` and a non-empty string `sig`;
   - `payload` is not a string of strict base64.

   Decode the payload's text as in step 1. Deeper than 64 levels refuses
   `depth_limit`. Not JSON, a member named twice, or not an object refuses
   `not_an_envelope`. Keep the payload's exact bytes `B` for step 9.
4. **Containment** (the signed route's answer). If the served `receipt` is not the
   same JSON value as the payload, refuse `different_document`. From here on, the
   receipt is the payload.
5. **Version.** If `receipt_version` is absent, not a string, or not a version
   section 8 lists, refuse `unknown_version`. Never read an unknown version as the
   nearest known one. If the receipt came in an envelope and its version is one no
   route ever signed (every version before 3.1; section 8.3), refuse `never_signed`:
   the signature was not made by athena-backend's signed route, whoever holds the
   key.
6. **Members.** Each of these refuses `malformed`:
   - the receipt has members other than exactly the ones its version defines for
     its form (section 8.3);
   - a 2.0 receipt's `coverage` is not exactly one of the two shapes 2.0 was
     emitted in: the five members #56 gave it, or the nine #67 gave it (section
     8.3). The shape it has is the one it is read and named as; a mixture neither
     PR emitted is read as neither;
   - `algorithm` is not `sha256`;
   - `digest` is not 64 lowercase hex;
   - in the full form from 3.1, `signed` is not `false`, `signature` not `null`,
     or `unsigned_reason` empty;
   - a 5.0 or 6.0 receipt's `authority_chains` is not exactly the shape section 4.10
     gives: its seven members, every verdict and basis in its censuses, each chain's
     four members and each hop's five, every relation, kind and verdict from the
     vocabulary of 4.10, a `digest` that is `sha256:` and 64 hex, counts that count
     the chains listed -- or a chain whose `verdict` is not the worst of its hops'.
     A verifier holds no graph and cannot re-derive a hop's verdict; it can tell that
     a chain read `proven` over an `unproven` hop is not one the backend emitted;
   - a 6.0 receipt's `consequential_effects` is not exactly the shape section 4.11
     gives: its three members, every status in its census, each effect's six
     members, a tool kind, class and status from the vocabulary of 4.11 with a
     status its class allows, a `digest` that is `sha256:` and 64 hex, counts that
     count the effects listed -- or a consequential effect `covered` while naming no
     chain (or `missing` while naming one), or, when `authority_chains` lists every
     chain in force, an effect naming a chain it does not list;
   - in the signed form from 4.1, `issued_at` is not a string in the one form of
     section 3 naming a real time (a month 13, a fraction, an offset other than
     `Z` or a number are all refused);
   - any number has a fraction or an exponent.
7. **Digest.** Recompute 5.2. If it differs from `digest`, refuse
   `digest_mismatch`.
8. **Evidence** (when an evidence file is given). If its `deployment` is not
   `system.uuid`, or the root recomputed by 5.3 is not `evidence.root`, or its
   finding count is not `evidence.finding_count`, refuse `evidence_mismatch`.
   Without an evidence file the root is bound by the digest and the signature but
   not re-derived, and the verifier says so.
9. **Signature.** With no envelope, refuse `unsigned`: every check above passed,
   which shows only that the copy is consistent. With an envelope and no key given,
   the verifier cannot run (exit status 2). Otherwise, for each signature entry:
   - its `keyid` is not a key you supplied: `wrong_key`;
   - that key's status is `revoked`: `revoked_key`;
   - Ed25519 verification of the base64-decoded `sig` over `PAE(T, B)`, with the
     key's material, fails (or `sig` is not base64): `bad_signature`;
   - one entry that verifies: go on to step 10.

   When no entry verifies, the reason is the last entry's, as the engine's own
   verifier reports it. An `issued_at` edited after signing changes `B`, so it
   refuses `bad_signature` here.
10. **Freshness** (only when a maximum age is given). The time is read only now:
    before the signature checks out, it is anyone's.
    - The receipt's version carries no signed time (4.0 and older): refuse
      `no_signed_time`. Its age cannot be read, so it cannot be shown to be fresh.
    - The verifier's clock minus `issued_at` is more than the maximum age: refuse
      `stale`.
    - Otherwise, **VERIFIED**.

    An `issued_at` ahead of the verifier's clock is not refused: one of the two
    clocks is wrong, and the verifier cannot tell which. It says so (section 9).
    With no maximum age, step 10 applies no policy, and a receipt that passed step 9
    is **VERIFIED**.

The refusal list. The verifier's `REFUSALS`, and the conformance vectors, are tested
against this table:

**What the backend's own code checks of this.** Steps 1 (for an envelope's payload),
3 and 4 are `envelope_over`. Step 5 is `receipt_schema`, which raises
`UnknownReceiptVersion`, and `NEVER_SIGNED`. Step 6 is the published schema's
members and constants, one level into each object member, with 2.0's `coverage`
exactly one branch of its `oneOf`, 5.0's `authority_chains` read by
`authority_chains_problem` and 6.0's `consequential_effects` by
`consequential_effects_problem` (`assurance/receipt.py`). Step 7 is `_digest`, and step 8 is
`deployment_receipt` over the stored evidence.
The backend makes no step-9 or step-10 check: it holds no keyring, and how old is
too old is the reader's policy. The conformance tests hold the verifier to the
backend's answer on every vector where the backend has one, and to the ground truth
(which key signed which bytes, and when the backend's clock said they were issued)
where the only defect is the signature or the age.

## 8. Versions

### 8.1 A version the reader does not know

A reader refuses it, naming it, and never reads it as the nearest version it does
know. The backend does the same when asked to describe one
(`UnknownReceiptVersion`, `assurance/receipt.py:1013-1041`): "a wrong schema is worse
than no schema". Note that the backend's `receipt_schema(None)` returns the
current schema, so a caller passing `receipt.get("receipt_version")` for a receipt
with no version gets 6.0's. The verifier refuses such a receipt instead.

**The digest across a MINOR step.** `receipt_version` is itself hashed (5.2), so
every version step changes the digest of an unchanged state, MINOR ones included.
A MINOR step adds, removes or retypes no hashed member: the digest a 4.1 receipt
gives a state is the one a 4.0 receipt gives it with `receipt_version` changed.
v4.0.md's history said "identical" for 3.1, which was true of neither of the two
values that moved then. `receipt_version` was one. `policy_version` was the other:
the assurance policy pin then embedded the version the receipt is stamped with, so
3.1 moved the pin, and with it every claim bound to it. From 4.1 the pin embeds the
version that introduced the hashed content instead (`HASHED_CONTENT_VERSION`,
`assurance/receipt.py:274-281`; `assurance/policy.py:108`), which a MINOR step
leaves where it is. 4.1 left every pin, and so every `policy_version`, unchanged.

**5.0 is MAJOR.** `authority_chains` is new hashed content, so a 4.1 digest and a 5.0
digest of the same state differ by more than the version string, and
`HASHED_CONTENT_VERSION` is 5.0's. That moved the pin, as a MAJOR step does -- and the
pin moved anyway, because the same change added two caps to the decision's rules
(`authority_chain_broken`, `authority_chain_unproven`) and the authority-chain rule
itself to the policy document.

**6.0 is MAJOR.** `consequential_effects` is new hashed content, so a 5.0 digest and a
6.0 digest of the same state differ by more than the version string, and
`HASHED_CONTENT_VERSION` is 6.0's. It is not a 5.1: a MINOR step changes only what
sits outside the digest (8.4, rule 2), and a reader told the two shapes were
compatible would compare digests that are not comparable. The pin moved anyway: the
same change added two caps to the decision's rules (`authority_chain_missing`,
`effect_class_unknown`) and the rule for which effects need a chain to the policy
document. It also changed what an unchanged deployment decides: an approved
workflow whose consequential effect no chain names read as if chains did not exist
under 5.0, and reads `needs_more_evidence` under 6.0.

### 8.4 The rule for the next version

1. A change to hashed content bumps the MAJOR number. That covers a member added,
   removed, renamed or retyped anywhere inside `H`, at any depth.
2. A change only to members outside the digest bumps the MINOR number (3.0 to 3.1,
   4.0 to 4.1). No hashed member changes; the digest of an unchanged state still
   moves, by the version string alone (8.2).
3. A version string names one shape. It is never reused for a different one, as
   2.0 was.
4. Each version gets its own file, `docs/receipt-spec/v<major>.<minor>.md`. Older
   files stay, and `docs/receipt-spec/README.md` lists them all. The conformance
   tests fail while `RECEIPT_VERSION` has no file.
5. The backend keeps every older version describable (`_ADDED_IN`, `receipt_schema`),
   and the verifier's `HASHED` table gains the new version. The conformance tests
   fail if the verifier and the backend's schemas disagree about any version.
6. The envelope (`envelope_version` 1, payload type `T`) is versioned separately
   from the receipt. A receipt version change does not change it.
7. The assurance policy pins `HASHED_CONTENT_VERSION`, not `RECEIPT_VERSION`. A
   MAJOR step moves it, and so moves the pin; a MINOR step moves neither.

## 9. The offline verifier

`tools/verify_receipt.py` is written from this specification, not from the
backend's code, and imports nothing from this repository. It needs the Python
standard library and, to check a signature, `cryptography` (Ed25519), the one crypto
library the backend already depends on. CI runs it on Python 3.11. Copy it anywhere.

```
python tools/verify_receipt.py RECEIPT.json --keyring KEYRING.json [--evidence EVIDENCE.json] [--max-age SECONDS]
python tools/verify_receipt.py RECEIPT.json --public-key KEY.b64 [--evidence EVIDENCE.json] [--max-age SECONDS]
python tools/verify_receipt.py RECEIPT.json      # the unsigned copy: checked, then refused as unsigned
```

- `RECEIPT.json` is the signed route's answer, the unsigned copy, or an envelope on
  its own, saved as served.
- `--keyring` is the engine's published keyring (6.4). Entries are checked when it
  is loaded: an id that is not its key's digest, a key enrolled twice or an unknown
  status make the keyring unusable.
- `--public-key` is a file holding one base64 Ed25519 public key. It is treated as
  an `active` key under its derived id, so it carries no revocation: use the
  keyring where you can.
- `--evidence` is the evidence file of 5.3.
- `--max-age SECONDS` is your freshness policy (step 10): a receipt issued longer
  ago than that, by its signed `issued_at` and this machine's clock, is refused
  `stale`, and one that carries no signed time (4.0 and older) is refused
  `no_signed_time`. It is a whole number of seconds, 0 or more.

Output. The first line is `VERIFIED` or `REFUSED <reason>`, with `<reason>` from the
table in section 7, and the lines after it say why. A verified receipt's summary
names the version and who emitted it, system, decision, digest, signer and status,
the issue time, what its version lacks, and whether the evidence root was
re-derived. The version is shown as `receipt   <version>, as emitted by <who, when>`
(section 8.3; for 2.0, the PR whose shape it has), and each group of members its
version lacks against 6.0 as `lacks     <members>: <what a receipt without them
cannot say>`. A 5.0 or 6.0 receipt's authority chains are summarised as `authority N chain(s)
in force: P proven, U unproven, B broken`, and every chain that is not proven gets a
line of its own naming the hops that hold it: `chain     <digest> <verdict> (<basis>):
hop <i> <relation> <verdict> (<codes>); ...`. A 6.0 receipt's effects are summarised
as `effects   N approved: C covered, M missing a chain, U of an unknown class, R
read-only`, and every effect that is missing a chain or of an unknown class gets a line
of its own: `effect    <digest> <status> (<tool kind>, <codes>)`. A refusal of an older version, once its version is read, names it and
lists what it lacks the same way. The issue time is shown as:

- `issued at <issued_at> by the issuer's clock, N s before this machine's clock` for
  a 4.1, 5.0 or 6.0 receipt;
- `issued at <issued_at> by the issuer's clock, N s AHEAD of this machine's clock:
  one of the two clocks is wrong` when it is later than this machine's clock. It is
  never shown as an age of 0;
- `issued at not signed (4.0): a 4.0 receipt carries no signing time` for a 4.0
  receipt (and the same for an older one, with its version). Never a time.

| Exit status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 0 | Verified. |
| 1 | Refused; the first line names the reason. |
| 2 | Could not run: bad arguments (a `--max-age` that is not a whole number of seconds, 0 or more, among them), an unreadable file, a keyring or evidence file not in the specified form, or a signed receipt with no key given. The message is on standard error and starts `CANNOT RUN:`, or is argparse's own usage error for an argument it cannot parse. |

The conformance vectors are the gate. Beside them,
`tests/test_a_signed_receipt_says_when_it_was_issued.py` signs through the real
route with a stand-in that signs as athena-engine's `wrap` does, and checks that
the time is in the signed bytes, that an edited time fails the signature, that two
signings of one state carry one digest, that a 4.0 receipt still verifies, and that
the policy pin did not move. `tests/test_every_receipt_version_ever_emitted_reads_back.py`
reads the receipts the old commits emitted (8.3): 1.0 and both 2.0 shapes read back,
named, by the backend's schema and the verifier alike; a 2.0 `coverage` in neither
shape is refused; a signed claim of a version no route signed is refused.

## 10. Known limitations of 6.0

Said here so nothing in a receipt reads as more than it is:

1. **The signed time is the issuer's clock.** `issued_at` is athena-backend's clock
   when it had the receipt signed. Nothing checks that clock, and it is not proof
   of when the state held. A backend clock set ahead would make its receipts look
   fresher for as long as it is ahead; the verifier shows a time ahead of its own
   clock as that, and does not refuse it.
2. **A 4.0 or older signed receipt carries no time**, and verifies for ever while
   its key is not revoked. A reader with a freshness policy refuses it
   (`no_signed_time`); a reader without one cannot tell its age.
3. **Nested members are checked in two places.** The verifier checks every
   version's top-level members and, below the top level, only 2.0's `coverage`,
   where one version string was emitted in two shapes, 5.0's `authority_chains`
   (4.10) and 6.0's `consequential_effects` (4.11). Everywhere else a nested member is bound by the digest but not held to
   its version's list. The backend's
   published schemas describe every object member one level down, and the
   conformance tests read them there.
4. **Genuine vectors exist for 1.0 and 2.0 only.** The old commits' own output is
   recorded for 1.0 and for both 2.0 shapes (8.3). The checks of 1.1, 3.0, 3.1, 4.0,
   4.1 and 5.0 read receipts derived from the current one by taking away what each later
   version added, which is how their schemas are derived too.
5. **The fingerprint comes only from the incident pack.** The finding list serves
   the uuid and the evidence rows of each leaf but not the fingerprint. The module
   docstring says an auditor can recompute a finding's digest from "classification,
   source and content hash, all of which the API already returns"
   (`assurance/receipt.py:6-9`); the fingerprint is needed too (5.3).
6. **Two receipts say nothing about signatures.** The bare `receipt` route and each
   finding's `receipt` carry a prominent SHA-256 `digest` and no `signed` field.
   That is the reading 3.1 fixed for the full receipt.
7. **The keyring pointer names an engine path.** The signed route's `verify` text
   says "GET /api/assurance/keyring". That is the engine's route; the backend does
   not serve it. The client's `assurance_keyring()`, whose docstring says
   "Proxied", is called by no route.
8. **The decision has seven values**, while the schema's description of `result`
   says "six-state".
9. **The signed route's docstring still says the same state signs to the same
   envelope** (`assurance/views.py:861-867`). From 4.1 it signs to the same digest
   in an envelope that differs by its `issued_at`; section 6.1 is what the code
   does.
10. **Every hop of a chain that starts at a person is provable, and every proof is
    Mythos-witnessed.** `produces` is proven by an `observed_effect` Achilles signs with a
    key of its own when the dispatch that carried the action out saw the provider
    answer success to exactly the permitted action -- witnessed by the dispatch path,
    not by a party independent of Achilles, and a provider's 2xx, not what the
    provider then did. `authenticated_as` and `delegates_to` are proven by signed
    `authentication` and `delegation` records (`assurance/identity_evidence.py`),
    signed by the Mythos-run collectors that read the customer's identity provider
    (`mythos-signin-collector`, `mythos-grant-collector`), each record carrying
    `witness: mythos`; a customer-run or third-party collector is planned. No basis
    rule reads Mythos-witnessed evidence as weaker, so a Mythos-witnessed record
    proves its hop; the witness is named in what proves it. Both are placed against
    the effect's instant, the observed effect's: a chain with no observed effect
    leaves them `unproven` (`effect_instant_unknown`). A delegation's revocation is
    read at that instant, so a revocation recorded after the receipt can turn a
    proven hop unproven in the next receipt (limitation 11). The ten reading codes
    `effect_instant_unknown`, `no_authentication_record`,
    `authentication_out_of_window`, `authentication_expired`, `no_delegation_record`,
    `delegation_action_unnamed`, `delegation_out_of_scope`,
    `delegation_out_of_window`, `delegation_expired` and `delegation_revoked`, and the
    two proving codes `authentication` and `delegation`, were added without a change to
    the receipt's shape; `no_record` is no longer emitted by either relation, and stays
    published for receipts that carry it. The seven reading codes
    `effect_evidence_unread`, `effect_tool_unnamed`, `effect_other_tool`,
    `effect_action_unnamed`, `effect_other_action`, `effect_other_dispatch` and
    `effect_outcome_cited_twice` were added without a change to the receipt's shape:
    a hop's `readings` already carried its codes. **The current producer of an
    `observed_effect` is Achilles' own dispatch path -- Mythos-witnessed, per the
    owner's 5 Oct scope call -- not an independent collector.** The 6.0 schema's
    descriptions that say "an independent collector" are left as published. Part 5
    added the three proving codes `approval_in_force_at_dispatch`,
    `contract_in_force_at_dispatch` and `route_at_dispatch` and the seven reading codes
    `dispatch_state_unrecorded`, `dispatched_under_superseded_policy`,
    `dispatched_under_superseded_contract`, `dispatched_on_another_route`,
    `effect_outside_dispatch_window`, `authentication_not_dispatched` and
    `delegation_not_dispatched`, without a change to the receipt's shape; `approval_in_force`,
    `approved_contract_in_force`, `superseded_contract`, `route_moved` and
    `route_unrecorded` are no longer emitted, and stay published for receipts that carry
    them. The approval digest, contracts, route and identity digests in a `v2` document
    are what the dispatch was PRESENTED under: Achilles signs them and cannot judge them,
    and the backend proves them against its own history. The graph hops' follow-up added
    the four proving codes `reach_in_force_at_dispatch`, `identity_in_force_at_dispatch`,
    `action_in_force_at_dispatch` and `within_approval_at_dispatch` and the three reading
    codes `reach_not_in_force_at_dispatch`, `identity_not_in_force_at_dispatch` and
    `action_not_in_force_at_dispatch`, without a change to the receipt's shape;
    `declared_edge`, `effective_reach`, `declared_identity`, `declared_permission`,
    `within_approval`, `inferred_edge`, `no_declared_identity` and
    `permissions_undeclared` are no longer emitted, and stay published for receipts that
    carry them. `acts_as_another`, `unreachable`, `permission_not_declared`,
    `identity_unresolved` and `dangling_edge` are still emitted, with the same verdicts,
    read at the dispatch instant instead of live: a contradiction at the dispatch is
    `broken`, and holds the decision at `needs_remediation`, as it did. A chain that
    records no dispatch is still broken by a contradiction in the graph as it stands
    (the same codes, and `outside_approval` against the approval as it stands), and is
    never proven by it: its graph hops otherwise read `dispatch_state_unrecorded`.
11. **A hop's verdict is the record as it stood when the receipt was computed, except
    what is read as of dispatch.** The approval, the tool contracts and the served route
    that `under_policy` and `invokes` read, and the graph edges -- identity, permission,
    reach -- and the approval's permissions that `through_identity`, `performs` and the
    reach read, are the ones in force at the dispatch instant (section 4.10), so a
    re-approval, a contract change, a route move or an edge changed after the effect does
    not move them. Which component each node names, shadow and coverage, and every
    signature's standing under the keyring are read live, so the same chain can still
    read `proven` in one receipt and `unproven` in the next with nobody touching it -- a
    component renamed or reclassified, a key withdrawn. The graph's history names
    components by their asset row and keeps how each was named: **a component
    re-created, renamed or deleted since the dispatch reads `unproven`, never `broken`**
    -- the history cannot say the component the chain names now did otherwise then.
    A rename that makes the chain's reference name, now, the very row the history shows
    in force reads `proven`, as the live rule would: which row a reference names is
    read live. **The histories record when the
    platform NOTICED a change, not when the customer made it.** The approval history and
    the graph's edge history are noted in the transaction of the write that moves them,
    or at the next decision refresh for a write no signal sees (a bulk update, a data
    migration); a tool contract when the platform records it. A change between its making
    and its noting is read as not yet made: an edge the customer removed before a
    dispatch, and the platform noticed only after it, reads as in force at that dispatch.
    A deployment's edge history begins at its first note, so a dispatch before then reads
    `dispatch_state_unrecorded`. The receipt attests the reading, and the authority-chains
    route serves the reading now.
12. **The verifier cannot re-derive a verdict.** It holds no graph, approval or
    outcome; it checks each chain reads as its worst hop and that the counts count the
    chains listed. A backend that read a hop wrongly signs that reading.
13. **At most 50 chains are listed.** The rest are counted in
    `authority_chains.not_shown` and served by the authority-chains route.
14. **An effect's class is what the tool declares.** `consequential_effects` reads
    the effect class and annotations the tool's registration declares; nothing
    observes what the tool does. A tool declared `read` that writes reads
    `not_required`. Declaring a class changes the tool's contract, so an approval
    bound to it before reads superseded until it is bound again; that is the only
    check on a declaration.
15. **Reach is read from the record, which has no workflow-to-tool edge.** An
    approval that binds no tools reaches every registered tool, and a tool no approval
    binds is an effect of no named workflow. A workflow that binds SOME tools is read
    as reaching those; a tool it reaches that another approval binds is that
    approval's effect, because nothing on record tells the two apart. A deployment
    with no approved workflow has no effect here.
16. **At most 50 effects are listed.** The rest are counted in
    `consequential_effects.not_shown` and served by the authority-chains route.
